Spacetime Matrix

Nine ways to move

Actions have a size in space and a duration in time, each at one of three levels: micro (<1 cm · <0.5 s), meso (1–100 cm · 0.5–10 s) or macro (>1 m · >10 s). Crossing them gives the spatial–temporal matrix of nine action types, from a micro–micro tremor to a macro–macro full-body sweep (Jensenius 2015).

Explore: move the phone and the dot shows where your action sits in the matrix. The sound is the map: small actions ring high and thin, large actions hum low; fast actions shimmer, slower ones breathe, and very slow ones hold a steady tone.

Practice: tap a cell to make it the target — sound only lives while you move at that size and that speed. Tour visits five cells, saving the hardest for last: the corner combinations. Micro–macro is special: a true micro–macro action moves less than a millimetre per second, below what a phone can sense — it appears as a state of standstill, so you win that cell by making the phone read you as still, and the stillness itself sings.

The phone estimates action size and period from its accelerometer and gyroscope, so the values are approximate on purpose — the levels are zones, not walls. On a computer without motion sensors, drag the dot to play the matrix directly.
